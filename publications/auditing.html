Auditing Frame-Level AUC in Weakly Supervised Video Anomaly Detection: Granularity, Resolution, and Scene Bias

Sara Abdulaziz and Egor Bondarev

Preprint · 2026

This paper audits the standard frame-level AUC evaluation protocol used in weakly supervised video anomaly detection. Using UCF-Crime and a diverse set of recent WSVAD models, the study evaluates the same model predictions at three granularities: global, per anomaly category, and within-video.

The analysis shows that pooled AUC does not reliably reflect within-video anomaly localization and often lacks the statistical resolution needed to distinguish models at the small margins commonly reported as state-of-the-art improvements. A paired video bootstrap is used to assess whether model rankings are statistically resolvable.

The paper further audits scene reliance using normal footage and scene-factor annotations for UCF-Crime. The results show that the examined models respond systematically to recording and semantic properties such as spatial resolution, color encoding, and location, highlighting the role of scene bias in current WSVAD evaluation.
