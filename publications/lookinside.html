Look Inside Each Video: Rethinking How Video Anomaly Detection Is Evaluated

Inpyo Song and Jangwon Lee

Progressive Journal · 2026

This paper reexamines the standard pooled frame-level Micro-AUROC evaluation protocol used in video anomaly detection. Micro-AUROC pools anomaly scores from all test videos, creating comparisons both between frames from the same video and between frames from different videos. The paper argues that these two types of comparisons measure fundamentally different aspects of detector behavior.

By decomposing Micro-AUROC into within-video and cross-video comparisons, the study shows that within-video anomalous-normal frame pairs constitute only 0.071–0.388% of all evaluated pairs across four widely used weakly supervised VAD benchmarks. Consequently, pooled Micro-AUROC is overwhelmingly determined by cross-video comparisons rather than by whether a detector correctly ranks anomalous frames above normal frames within the same video.

The experiments demonstrate this distinction directly. Video-level classifiers that assign a single score to every frame of a video achieve 81.40–97.18% Micro-AUROC despite obtaining exactly 50% Within-AUROC. Similarly, replacing the frame scores of existing weakly supervised VAD detectors with each video's mean score removes within-video temporal ranking while retaining a median 98.8% of the original Micro-AUROC margin above chance across 120 runs.

These results show that high pooled Micro-AUROC alone does not establish successful temporal anomaly localization. Because the issue arises from the evaluation protocol rather than the form of training supervision, the distinction applies broadly whenever frame-level VAD scores are pooled across multiple videos. The paper therefore motivates reporting within-video ranking metrics alongside Micro-AUROC.
